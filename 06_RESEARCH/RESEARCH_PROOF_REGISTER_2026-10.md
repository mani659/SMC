### B3. High-EV vs premature (each proposed next step, judged)

- **Near-miss/counterfactual on unplaced routes + blocked candidates: HIGH-EV, do first.** The only proposed work that can change a strategic belief with data on disk (protective vs destructive gates). Paper-trade the 10 routes' recorded entry/SL/TP with frozen fill semantics; extend to session/R7-blocked candidates. Cheap, decisive, no code changes to product.
- **Coincidence count (confirmations coinciding with validated-unarmed zones in-window): HIGH-EV, cheapest.** A pure counting question that prices the order-constraint: dozens = sequence redesign territory (Architect ruling with evidence); single digits = the constraint binds nothing measurable — stop redesigning sequence, focus entry/selectivity. No code, artifacts on disk.
- **Episode-store design lock: VALUABLE BUT PREMATURE as #2.** It kills real costs (re-derived joins bit us three times this session: float-vs-string identity, aliased detector lists, first-touch stuck at zero) — but it is a refactor across the hottest paths (detection/arm/scan) with byte-identity downstream expectations, and its core input (episode identity grain) is exactly what A4 left weakest (SAME 0). Locking the ontology before identity settles risks baking in the wrong grain. Do it third, with a byte-identity migration proof, not second.
- **Regime stratification as a standalone: PREMATURE until n exists** — except on fragment-level proxies (trigger completions per month × displacement presence), where n already exists. As a route-level study it is texture-only by construction.
- **CHOCH/FVG-fill research gates: correctly banned as gates; MEDIUM as measurement.** CHOCH 15.5% is the one structure signal that doesn't saturate — understanding why it's rare is legitimate curiosity, but with n≈27 it cannot carry a decision. FVG fill-through (vs touch) needs a frozen definition first.
- **Loosening thresholds for n: REJECTED outright.** It manufactures sample size while destroying the measurement baseline, and every loosened gate would need OOS that doesn't exist. The program's n problem is solved by longer windows + fragment proxies, never by widening gates.

### B4. Challenge + recommendation (dissent recorded)

The Architect's preferred order is near-miss → episode-store lock → coincidence count. **I agree on near-miss first and dissent on the rest: coincidence count should be second, episode-store third.** Defense: (1) cost — coincidence is hours of counting on disk vs weeks of refactor + migration proof across detection/arm/scan with 1011 tests watching; (2) decision value — coincidence directly prices whether the sequential-order constraint (the subject of the entire incubator debate) binds anything measurable, while the store answers no open question by itself; (3) sequencing risk — the store's identity grain depends on open A4 questions (SAME 0, partials contested), so building it now means building on the weakest finding. The store remains the right third step: after coincidence tells us whether sequence matters and near-miss tells us whether gates destroy, the store makes all future measurement cheaper and safer. **CRITICAL_DISSENT: YES — coincidence count second, episode-store third (evidence and cost above).**
